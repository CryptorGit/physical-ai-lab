# One Policy, Two Gaits: Single-Checkpoint Bidirectional Locomotion for Unitree G1

This simulation study investigated whether qualitatively different walking and running contact patterns could be integrated into a single Unitree G1 policy without runtime expert routing, checkpoint switching, or action blending. Early attempts used only a speed command and optimized a shared policy across standing, walking, running, and transition regimes. They retained walking but failed to consolidate running into the deterministic mean policy. Completion events appeared under stochastic exploration, yet event-stratified on-policy sampling and short-horizon replay did not transfer those events into a stable running attractor.

A reverse continuation from a running parent tracked 1.2 m/s accurately but retained a running contact cycle instead of recovering walking. Diagnostic comparisons at the same 1.2 m/s showed that walking and running occupied nearly disjoint state distributions (nonlinear AUROC ≈ 0.999986), differed substantially in action space, and were not connected by bounded local perturbations. Walking also had a higher offline return by approximately 2.9, indicating a dynamical and optimization barrier rather than reward indifference.

We therefore augmented the original 123-dimensional observation with one continuous scalar gait command, yielding a 124-dimensional input to a single `124–256–128–128–37` mean network. Endpoint supervision produced one checkpoint that selected walking at `gait_cmd=0` and running at `gait_cmd=1`, including at the same target speed. Deterministic evaluations achieved 100% walking at 1.2 m/s, 100% running at 1.2, 2.4, and 2.6 m/s, and 100% bidirectional WALK↔RUN transitions.

Because the walking and running teachers used different exploration covariances, we introduced one gait-conditioned diagonal Gaussian head and calibrated gait-specific exploration temperatures. Safe candidates were `alpha_walk=0.30` and `alpha_run=0.65`; these retained 100% walking, 97% low-speed running, and 100% transitions in the calibrated stochastic evaluation. Continued PPO with soft endpoint KL anchors nevertheless accumulated semantic drift, and optimizer-moment adaptation did not resolve it.

Final supervised integration extended the same actor to walking from 0.6–1.2 m/s and running from 1.2–2.6 m/s, with successful acceleration, deceleration, and practical stopping. The strict static-standing gate remained unresolved: the final speed averaged approximately 0.055 m/s, but small stepping/contact oscillations violated zero-flight and final-double-support requirements. Specialist standing positive controls also failed this strict gate. The result is simulation-only and demonstrates single-policy gait selection and bidirectional transition, not perfect standing, scratch-only acquisition, Sim2Real, or general humanoid control.
